# Decision Making Cannot Be Outsourced

I think the most important skill to build now is the ability to make good decisions quickly.

With AI, you can keep asking for another comparison, another plan, another recommendation. But at some point, somebody has to decide what happens next. The temptation is to let the model make that call too. If it turns out badly, though, you are still responsible. Accepting its recommendation is itself a decision.

The model I find useful is how a CEO works with a team. The team brings research, context, and recommendations. The CEO has to weigh that input, decide which trade-offs the company can live with, and take responsibility for the direction. I think we need to approach AI in the same way. Let it do the analysis. Use that analysis to make a decision you understand and can stand behind.

That starts with defining what you are trying to achieve and the constraints that matter. Ask AI to compare the options, surface assumptions, and make the strongest case against its own recommendation. Check the evidence behind the claims that could change your choice. Then make the call. If you cannot explain why you chose something without referring back to the chat, you probably need to understand the decision better.

Suppose you are deciding whether to build a feature a customer has requested. AI can help estimate the effort and compare possible approaches. You still have to decide how much that customer's need should influence the product, what other work gets delayed, and whether your team can maintain what you will ship. A detailed implementation plan cannot settle those priorities for you.

This is where separating signal from noise becomes important. A long answer can make a weak assumption feel settled. A confident recommendation can distract you from the one fact that would change the decision. Ask yourself what evidence would make you choose differently. That tells you where to spend your attention, and whether another round of analysis would actually help.

Making decisions quickly also requires knowing how much analysis a decision deserves. If you can reverse it cheaply, make a call, try it, and learn. If it is difficult to undo, spend more time checking the assumptions that could make it fail. Waiting has a cost too. You need to recognize when you have enough information to act, even while some uncertainty remains.

Judgment improves when you come back to your decisions. Write down why you made the call and what you expected to happen. Later, compare that with the result. If you were wrong, work out which assumption failed. If you were right, check whether your reasoning held up or you got lucky. This is how you build the experience to make the next decision faster.

Let AI do the analysis. The decision, and the responsibility for it, are yours.
